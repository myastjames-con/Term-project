Western chorus frog habitats and conservation areas

The Western chorus frog is a small amphibian that breeds in shallow wetlands. Amphibians are beneficial to our ecosystem since they help cycle nutrients, shape food webs, and reduce mosquito numbers from wetlands, (Hocking & Babbitt, 2014). In the The Great Lakes / St. Lawrence Western Chorus frog population (Canadian Shield population), which includes my study area of Montérégie, they are listed as a threatened specie (Environment and Climate Change Canada, n.d.). Wetlands cover only 4.9% of Montérégie (578 km²), and suitable habitat for the La Prairie population fell by 57.3% between 1992 and 2013 due to housing development (Environment and Climate Change Canada, 2015). On my map, 10 of 56 recorded habitat sites overlap a protected area.

This map is for amphibian conservation groups.
